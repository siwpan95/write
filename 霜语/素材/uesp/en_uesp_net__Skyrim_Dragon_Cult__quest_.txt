The [[Lore:Dragon Cult|Dragon Cult]], also known as the '''Atmoran Dragon Cult'''{{ref|name=TESVSPOGG|{{Cite Book|The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim: Prima Official Game Guide|ns_base=Books}}}}{{rp|471}} or '''Cult of the Dragon Priests''',{{ref|name=TESVSPOGG}}{{rp|472}} was the central religious authority on the continent of [[Lore:Atmora|Atmora]] and in the [[Lore:Tamriel|Tamrielic]] province of [[Lore:Skyrim|Skyrim]] during the Late [[Lore:Merethic Era|Merethic Era]], before the [[Lore:Dragon War|Dragon War]]. The cult was based around the worship of dragons and the rule of their intermediaries, the [[Lore:Dragon Priest|Dragon Priests]].{{ref|name=TDW|{{Cite Book|The Dragon War}}}} The dragons were ruled by [[Lore:Alduin|Alduin]], the First Dragon{{ref|{{Cite Book|The Song of Gods}}}} and self-proclaimed Firstborn of Akatosh,{{ref|[[Skyrim:Alduin|Alduin]]'s dialogue in ''[[Skyrim:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} the creator of the dragon civilization{{ref|name=SI}} and of the Dragon Cult itself.{{ref|group=UOL|name=SIUP}}

When the Atmorans arrived on [[Lore:Tamriel|Tamriel]], they brought with them [[Lore:Nord#The Old Ways|the ancient totemic religion]] that placed the [[Lore:Dragon|Dragon]] totem at the top. The dragons acted as god-kings, lording over men via their intermediaries, the Dragon Priests. These Priests were granted authority equal to that of secular kings at the time,{{ref|name=TDW}} ruling over Skyrim and much of northern Tamriel,{{ref|name=ESODP}} and were allowed not only to invoke the name "Dragon" but also gifted with the immense powers of [[Lore:Thu'um|the Voice]].{{ref|name=ZtS|[[Online:Zaan the Scalecaller|Zaan the Scalecaller]]'s dialogue in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}}{{ref|name=TGATT}} 

Over time, for some unknown reason, the Dragon Priests in Skyrim began to become far more tyrannical than the ones in Atmora had been.{{ref|name=TDW|{{Cite Book|The Dragon War}}}} The dragons and their priests made slaves out of the populace, ruling with an iron fist from their capital in [[Lore:Labyrinthian|Bromjunaar]].{{ref|name=AMM}} Years of subjugation eventually led to the humans rebelling against their overlords in the legendary Dragon War,{{ref|name=TDW|{{Cite Book|The Dragon War}}}} culminating in the defeat of Alduin atop the [[Lore:Throat of the World|Throat of the World]] in a mythical battle.{{ref|name=TODC}}

The dragons' numbers were decimated during the Dragon War.{{ref|name=TBD|{{Cite Book|There Be Dragons}}}} Worship of Alduin was outlawed,{{ref|name=VOFN}} and in {{Year|1E 140}} the last stronghold of the original Dragon Cult, the monastery of [[Lore:Forelhost|Forelhost]], was found and besieged by [[Lore:Harald|Harald]], who would later become the first High King of Skyrim.{{ref|name=SSSJ|{{Cite Book|Skorm Snow-Strider's Journal}}}}{{ref|group=nb|''[[Lore:Skorm Snow-Strider's Journal|Skorm Snow-Strider's Journal]]'', dated to 1E 139-140 speaks of Harald not as "King Harald" or "High King Harald," but as "Lord Harald," indicating the events described in the journal took place before Harald's ascension to the throne in 1E 143.}}

==History==
[[File:ON-crown store-Frostbane Dragon Priest.jpg|right|thumb|Atmoran dragon priests, wearing garb designed to withstand extreme cold]][[File:ON-furnishing-Dragon Priest Frieze Triumph 02.png|left|thumb|180px|Dragon priest bas-relief]]
===Dragon Rule in Atmora and Tamriel===
While the dragon cult eventually established itself as the foremost religion in Atmora, it was not always as ubiquitous as it became, with its prominence likely being a later development than the traditional Atmoran totemic religion.{{ref|name=DATN|{{Cite Book|Divines and the Nords}}}} Dragons have existed since the [[Lore:Dawn Era|Dawn]] of time,{{ref|name=TBD}}{{ref|name=SI}}{{ref|{{Cite Book|Dread Their Return and Prepare}}}} but they were "wild" and "uncivilized" until Alduin brought order to them, creating civilization amongst the dragons.{{ref|name=SI|{{Cite Book|Shalidor's Insights}}}} This civilization formed the basis of the Dragon Cult in Atmora.{{ref|group=UOL|name=SIUP|[[Skyrim:Shalidor's Insights (unused pages)|Shalidor's Insights (unused pages)]]}} Some traditions speak of the Dragons ruling over all of [[Lore:Mundus|Mundus]] at this time, before the birth of Men.{{ref|name=Hrothgar}}

The Atmoran totemic pantheon emerged before the dragons subjugated humanity,{{ref|name=DATN}} though by the time of [[Lore:The Return|the Return]] it had become ubiquitous.{{ref|group=UOL|name=DWD}} Some sources imply that the dragons were attracted to the Men of Atmora because they shared the worldview of the world being a gift from the gods, though it has also been theorized that humans learned this worldview from the dragons.{{ref|group=UOL|name=SIUP}}

Alduin's Dragon Cult came to lord over humanity in Atmora. The dragons considered themselves god-kings over men, superior in every way to humanity, though they were uninterested in the petty politics of mankind. Thus they appointed dragon priests, granting the powers of the Thu'um to powerful mages and setting them up as intermediaries between themselves and humanity.{{ref|name=TDW}}{{ref|name=TGATT}} Dragons who chose for themselves Priests were called Dragon Lords, forming bonds with these mortals and granting them the power of the Thu'um.{{ref|name=ZtS}} It is unclear whether this bond was magical or simply political, though it is clear that at least in some cases there was a strong emotional bond between Dragon Lord and chosen priest.{{ref|name=THOZTS}}

During or soon after the Atmoran migrations to Tamriel, the Dragon Cult began to assert more influence and become much more malevolent.{{ref|name=TDW}} The cult ruled [[Lore:Skyrim|Skyrim]]{{ref|name=TDW}} and indeed all of northern Tamriel{{ref|name=ESODP|[[Online:Dragon Priest (costume)|Dragon Priest (costume)]] item description in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}} with an iron fist, effectively enslaving the populace.{{ref|name=TDW}}{{ref|name=Hrothgar|[[Skyrim:High Hrothgar#Etched Tablets|The Etched Tablets of High Hrothgar]]}} Some oral myths even speak of dragons ruling over the whole world around this time.{{ref|name=TGATT|{{Cite Book|The Guardian and the Traitor}}}}

For a time the Dragon Cult was led by [[Lore:Miraak|Miraak]],{{ref|name=Firstblade|[[ON:Firstblade Style|Firstblade Style]] bundle description in [[ESO:Online|ESO]]}} the first man to be [[Lore:Dragonborn|blessed with the very soul of a dragon]].{{ref|name=miraak}} At the height of the Dragon Cult's rule of Skyrim, the highest ranking Priests of the Cult would meet at [[Lore:Labyrinthian|Bromjunaar]] in modern-day [[Lore:Hjaalmarch|Hjaalmarch]] to discuss matters of their rule. Originally built as a temple to the dragons, Bromjunaar grew into a great city, eventually becoming the capital of the Dragon Cult's rule.{{ref|name=AMM|{{Cite Book|A Minor Maze}}}} The mask [[Lore:Dragon Priest Masks#Konahrik|Konahrik]] was stored there, and it could only be accessed if all eight of the lesser [[Lore:Dragon Priest Masks|masks]] of Skyrim were placed on their respective pedestals within its sanctuary.{{ref|[[Skyrim:Labyrinthian|Labyrinthian]] in ''[[Skyrim:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}

[[Lore:Skuldafn|Skuldafn]] was a highly significant location for the Dragon Cult, known as one of Alduin's high fanes{{ref|name=Odahviing|[[SR:Odahviing|Odahviing]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} and sometimes called the World-Eater's Eyrie,{{ref|[[Legends:World-Eater's Eyrie|World-Eater's Eyrie]] card in [[Legends:Legends|Legends]]}} or nest.{{ref|group=nb|The word "eyrie" means the nest of a bird, usually a bird of prey, on a cliff or mountaintop.}} From his perch at Skuldafn Alduin could enter a portal that led directly to Sovngarde to eat the souls of the dead, a privilege he guarded jealously.{{ref|name=Odahviing}} The Dragon Priests of Skuldafn (such as [[Lore:Nahkriin|Nahkriin]]) were said to have stood at Alduin's right hand,{{ref|name=EGTSkyrim|{{Cite Book|EGT|Skyrim}}}} with the staff Nahkriin wielded being a necessary key used to open the portal.{{ref|name=Skyrim}}

During the Late Merethic Era, most leaders of high stature were at least in some way connected to the Dragon Cult.{{ref|group=UOL|name=DWD}}  Heirlooms from around the time of the [[Lore:Ysgramor Dynasty|Ysgramor Dynasty]] were marked as being of royalty by the motif of a dragon head.{{ref|[[ON:Eastmarch Antiquities#Royal Ivory Hilt|Royal Ivory Hilt]] antiquities codex description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} One ancient chalice belonging to a member of the Ysgramor Dynasty before Harald featured [[Lore:Ysgramor|Ysgramor]] surrounded by draconic silhouettes,{{ref|name=SCOY|[[ON:Eastmarch Antiquities#Sacred Chalice of Ysgramor|Sacred Chalice of Ysgramor]] antiquities codex description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} and the historian [[Lore:Hasphat Antabolis|Hasphat Antabolis]] theorized that Ysgramor may have even been a dragon priest.{{ref|group=UOL|name=DWD}}{{NewLeft}}

===The Dragon War Begins===
Over time the Dragons and their priests began to become more and more tyrannical, making slaves of the populace. It is unknown exactly what led to this change, though scholars have posited theories including an ambitious dragon priest taking power, a particular dragon doing the same, or a series of weak kings. Whatever the case, the new status quo fermented discontent within the men of Tamriel.{{ref|name=TDW}}

Over time, the tyrannical rule of the dragon priests led Men to rebel. Several dragons joined the side of Men during the war against Alduin, most prominent among them being Alduin's own lieutenant [[Lore:Paarthurnax|Paarthurnax]].{{ref|name=AOD|{{Cite Book|Atlas of Dragons}}}}{{ref|name=Skyrim|Events of ''[[Skyrim:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} With the help of the goddess [[Lore:Kyne|Kyne]], Paarthurnax taught Men how to use the Thu'um,{{ref|name=Hrothgar}} which had previously been restricted to the Dragon Priests.{{ref|name=TGATT}} At some point in time after the Velothi exodus,{{ref|name=DG|[[ON:Darns Gilvio|Darns Gilvio]]'s dialogue in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}}{{ref|group=nb|At least one [[Lore:Chimer|Chimer]] warrior supposedly fought in the Dragon War alongside Hakon One-Eye, and their descendant Darns Gilvio would go on to begrudgingly become a member of a [[Lore:Sai Sahan|Sai Sahan]]'s [[Lore:Dragonguard|Dragonguard]]. This places the end of the Dragon War at some time past the Velothi exodus.}} the Atmoran hero [[Lore:Hakon One-Eye|Hakon One-Eye]], one of the first Tongues, attempted to enlist the help of Miraak, a [[Lore:Dragonborn|Dragonborn]] who would have been able to permanently kill dragons and stop them from being resurrected by Alduin, but he refused to join their cause.{{ref|name=miraak|[[Skyrim:Miraak (person)|Miraak]]'s dialogue in ''[[Skyrim:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}{{ref|group=nb|It is unknown whether or not Miraak was leader of the Dragon Cult when he was approached by Hakon and his group, or even whether or not he was a member, since it is unknown how long Hakon's quest lasted.}}
===Miraak's Descent===
[[File:MER-art-Sahrotaar And Miraak.webp|Miraak riding the dragon Sahrotaar|right|thumb]]{{Quote Box|style=float:none; max-width:none|''So you have slain Alduin... Well done. I could have slain him myself, back when I walked the earth, but I chose a different path.''|Miraak{{ref|name=miraak}}}}
Dragons are born with an innate urge to conquer, and Dragonborn mortals born with the soul of a dragon are no different.{{ref|name=PTHNX}} The first Dragonborn in history was Miraak, who had in his soul an extreme desire for power. Miraak was at one point the leader of the Dragon Cult,{{ref|name=Firstblade}}{{ref|group=nb|The timeline of when Miraak was the Dragon Cult's leader is unclear, as he makes no mention of it in his dialogue. It is, however, known that he had the sword Firstblade while leader of the Dragon Cult,{{ref|name=Firstblade}} and that he kept this sword all the way up until his death at the hands of Hermaeus Mora in the Fourth Era.{{ref|name=SKDB}}}} and he ruled over the island of [[Lore:Solstheim|Solstheim]] in the name of the dragons.{{ref|name=DBTrailer|[[General:The Elder Scrolls V Skyrim: Dragonborn - Official Trailer|The Elder Scrolls V Skyrim: Dragonborn - Official Trailer]]}} At some point before Alduin's defeat,{{ref|name=miraak}} Miraak, who sought to rule Solstheim not as an intermediary for the dragons but for himself,{{ref|name=Frea}} was seduced by the power that the [[Lore:Daedric Prince|Daedric Prince]] [[Lore:Hermaeus Mora|Hermaeus Mora]] offered him. Miraak, along with three other Priests, [[Lore:Ahzidal|Ahzidal]], [[Lore:Dukaan|Dukaan]], and [[Lore:Zahkriisos|Zahkriisos]], traveled to Solstheim to form a cult to the Prince at Miraak's personal temple at the center of the island.{{ref|name=TESVSPOGG}}{{rp|585}} The three Dragon Priests that followed Miraak to Solstheim were together called his Acolyte Priests.{{ref|name=TESVSPOGG}}{{rp|174}}

Hermaeus Mora taught Miraak a new Shout, one with the power to bend the will of mortals and dragons,{{ref|name=Mora|[[Skyrim:Hermaeus Mora|Hermaeus Mora]]'s dialogue in [[Skyrim|''Skyrim'']]}} a power Miraak used throughout his conquest.{{ref|name=SKDB|Events of [[Skyrim:Dragonborn|''Skyrim: Dragonborn'']]}} Mora also tempted Miraak into taking advantage of his innate Dragonborn ability to gain more and more personal power, and Miraak began killing dragons specifically in order to absorb their souls and gain their power.{{ref|name=LCL|[[Legends:Card Lore#Miraak, Dragonborn|Miraak, Dragonborn]] [[Legends:Card Lore|Card Lore]] in ''[[Legends:Legends|Legends]]''}}{{ref|name=DBTrailer}} He hung many of their bones up like personal trophies in his temple at the center of Solstheim.{{ref|Events of [[Skyrim:The Temple of Miraak|The Temple of Miraak]] in [[Skyrim:Skyrim|''Skyrim'']]}}{{ref|name=Frea}}

Another Dragon Priest, [[Lore:Vahlok the Jailor|Vahlok the Jailor]], uncovered Miraak's plan to betray his Dragon Lord, and confronted Miraak.{{ref|name=TGATT}} A great war was fought over this betrayal, with the orthodox Dragon Priests on one side and Miraak and his Acolyte Priests on the other.{{ref|name=LCL}}{{ref|name=TESVSPOGG}}{{rp|662}} Eventually Vahlok caught up to Miraak, and the two Dragon Priests engaged in a duel so vicious and destructive that the modern island of Solstheim was allegedly ripped from the mainland of [[Lore:Skyrim|Skyrim]].{{ref|name=TGATT|{{Cite Book|The Guardian and the Traitor}}}} The struggle lasted for many days, the two of them hurling spells and Shouts at each other, but it eventually ended with Miraak's defeat. Just as Vahlok was about to deliver the killing blow, though, Hermaeus Mora snatched away his champion into Apocrypha.{{ref|name=TGATT}} 

Following this, the dragons razed his temple to ashes.{{ref|name=Frea|[[Skyrim:Frea|Frea]]'s dialogue in [[Skyrim:Dragonborn|''Skyrim: Dragonborn'']]}}{{ref|name=TESVSPOGG}}{{rp|584}} and the Acolyte Priests were sealed within crypts.{{ref|name=SKDB}}{{ref|name=Ahzidal|{{Cite Book|Ahzidal's Descent}}}}{{ref|name=TESVSPOGG}}{{rp|1023}} Vahlok was tasked with watching Solstheim for signs of Miraak's return,{{ref|name=TGATT}} and his courage would go on to inspire both men and dragons.{{ref|name=Tharstan|[[Skyrim:Tharstan|Tharstan]]'s dialogue in [[Skyrim:Skyrim|''Skyrim'']]}}

===The Dragon War Ends===
[[File:LG-cardart-Cast Into Time 2.jpg|left|thumb|Alduin cast into time by Hakon One-Eye, Gormlaith Golden-Hilt, and Felldir the Old]]
{{Quote Box|style=float:none; max-width:none|''Hold, Alduin on the Wing! Sister Hawk, grant us your sacred breath to make this contract heard! Begone, World-Eater! By words with older bones than your own we break your perch on this age and send you out! You are banished! Alduin, we shout you out from all our endings unto the last!''|[[Lore:Felldir the Old|Felldir the Old]]}}

Three ancient Atmoran heroes, [[Lore:Hakon One-Eye|Hakon One-Eye]], [[Lore:Gormlaith Golden-Hilt|Gormlaith Golden-Hilt]], and [[Lore:Felldir the Old|Felldir the Old]], devised a plan to defeat Alduin.{{ref|name=Skyrim}} With the help of [[Lore:Paarthurnax|Paarthurnax]],{{ref|name=Alduin|[[Skyrim:Alduin|Alduin]]'s dialogue in [[Skyrim:Skyrim|''Skyrim'']]}} Hakon and his allies created a new Shout, called Dragonrend, which bound dragons to mortality and cast them down from the sky to be defeated. Without Miraak's help, though, they could not actually kill Alduin.{{ref|name=miraak}} The trio considered using an [[Lore:Elder Scroll|Elder Scroll]] to cast Alduin adrift upon the currents of time, and though Hakon initially protested to the plan, Felldir ultimately brought a Scroll to the Throat of the World and used it against Alduin when the chance presented itself.{{ref|Events of [[Skyrim:Alduin's Bane|Alduin's Bane]] in ''[[Skyrim:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} 

Though some hoped Alduin would be gone forever, Paarthurnax knew he would [[Lore:Return of the Dragons|eventually return]] at the same place he was banished from, and so stayed at the Throat of the World all the way until the advent of the Last Dragonborn.{{ref|name=Skyrim}}

The dragon priests were overthrown and dragons were slaughtered in large numbers before fleeing to remote areas. Though they had lost their god-kings and were cast out of mainstream [[Lore:Nord|Nord]]ic society, the dragon cult managed to adapt and survive for centuries. Whenever they could lay hands on the remains of a dragon, they would build a tomb for it known as a "dragon mound", a practice they maintained for centuries after the war.{{ref|name=TDW}}{{ref|name=AOD}} They believed that one day Alduin would return and raise the dragons from the dead,{{ref|Inscription on the back of the [[SR:Dragonstone|Dragonstone]] in [[SR:Skyrim|''Skyrim'']]}} where they would reward the faithful.{{ref|name=TDW}} The Dragon Cult was said to do horrible things at the sites of these mounds.{{ref|[[ON:Narir|Narir]]'s dialogue in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}}

The dragon cult was eventually driven into obscurity, surviving only in the form of draugr who continue to prowl ancient ruins.{{ref|name=TDW}} On the 21st of Sun's Dusk in {{Year|1E 139}}, a small Nordic army in service to [[Lore:Harald|Lord Harald]] stumbled upon the monastary of [[Lore:Forelhost|Forelhost]] during Harald's campaign to push the elves out of their native homeland. Within Forelhost they discovered a secret enclave of Dragon Cultists, who they believed to have been wiped out after the Dragon War. Thinking this too important to ignore, they abandoned their pursuit of the elves and immediately besieged the cultists. The siege lasted several weeks, ending on the 6th of Morning Star in {{Year|1E 140}}, after the cult committed mass suicide.{{Ref|name=SSSJ|{{Cite Book|Skorm Snow-Strider's Journal}}}}

===Legacy===
[[File:LG-back-Ebonheart Pact.png|right|thumb|The symbol of the Ebonheart Pact, inspired by the symbolism of the Dragon Cult]]{{Quote Box|style=float:none; max-width:none|''I stand before my ancestors, at this holy dragonshrine, to seal our pact for the greater good.''|[[Lore:Jorunn|Jorunn the Skald-King]]}}

Nordic religion in the centuries after the Dragon War had a very muddled relationship with dragons and their veneration. The veneration of dragons understandably dwindled among Nords after the Dragon War.{{ref|name=TDW}} Though direct worship of Alduin was made illegal,{{ref|name=VOFN|{{Cite Book|Varieties of Faith: The Nords}}}} some cultists still worshipped him in secret.{{ref|[[ON:Treasures D#Dragon Cult Walking Stick|Dragon Cult Walking Stick]] item description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} 

When faced with [[Lore:Imperial|Imperial]]ization, many Nords were reluctant to adopt the worship of Akatosh in part due of their negative history with dragon worship.{{Ref|name=APQ|{{Cite Book|Artorius Ponticus Answers Your Questions}}}} Many found the Imperial fascination with Akatosh to be both perplexing and mildly disturbing.{{ref|name=DATN}} Nord religion would eventually come to think of Alduin not only as the harbinger of the apocalypse but as the creator god, who destroyed the previous world in a firestorm to create this one.{{ref|name=VOF|{{Cite Book|Varieties of Faith in Tamriel}}}} Some Nords would begin every service in the temple with a prayer to praise Alduin, followed by another prayer to keep him asleep.{{ref|name=DATN}}

The Nords continued to venerate both the grand temples of the Dragon Cult and the burial mounds of the dragons themselves up into the Second Era, referring to them both as dragonshrines.{{ref|[[Online:Ritual at the Dragonshrine|Ritual at the Dragonshrine]] in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}}{{ref|name=Jorunn|[[Online:Jorunn|Jorunn the Skald-King]]'s dialogue in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}} They were revered by the Nords as holy places, the most sacred of them being [[Lore:Skuldafn|Skuldafn]].{{ref|name=ESOLoad|[[Online:Skuldafn|Skuldafn]]'s loading screen in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}} The draconic banners of the [[Lore:Ebonheart Pact|Ebonheart Pact]] during the Interregnum were inspired by the symbology of the Dragon Cult,{{ref|[[ON:Dragon Cult Body Tattoo|Dragon Cult Body Tattoo]] item description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} and [[Lore:Jorunn|Jorunn Skald-King]] intended to make the Pact official at the holy dragonshrine of Skuldafn. Though these initial plans for a Great Moot were thwarted, the Pact still ended up being solidified at Skuldafn, albeit not in as formal a way as Jorunn had intended.{{ref|name=SoS|Events of [[ON:Songs of Sovngarde|Songs of Sovngarde]] in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}}

The last time a dragon was seen in Skyrim before [[Lore:Return of the Dragons|Alduin's return]] in {{Year|4E201}} was sometime during the Third Era.{{ref|[[SR:Irileth|Irileth]]'s dialogue in [[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]}} Those who survived through the Dragon War and the crusades of the [[Lore:Akavir|Akaviri]] [[Lore:Dragonguard|Dragonguard]] seemingly vanished all at once, though it is unclear what happened to them. Some tales speak of a dragon king who devoured all of them rather than let mankind kill them, while other tales speak of Tiber Septim absorbing their essences when he ascended to godhood.{{ref|name=TBD}} Some dragons evidently survived through the centuries by going into hiding.{{ref|[[Blades:Shulkunaak|Shulkunaak]]'s appearance in [[Blades:Blades|Blades]]}}

By the time of Alduin's return in the Fourth Era, most details about the Dragon War had been completely lost to history.{{ref|name=Farengar|[[Skyrim:Farengar Secret-Fire|Farengar Secret-Fire]]'s dialogue in [[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]}} Dragons themselves had mostly been relegated to myth and legend, with some believing they were nothing more than stories.{{ref|[[Skyrim:Gerdur|Gerdur]]'s dialogue in ''[[Skyrim:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}{{NewLeft}}

===Survival===
[[File:ON-npc-Aspirant Yolstagg 02.jpg|left|thumb|Aspirant Yolstagg, leader of the resurging Dragon Cult in Dragonhome]]
{{Quote Box|style=float:none; max-width:none|''No one would look favorably on a resurgence of the Dragon Cult, no matter how laughable the effort.''|[[Online:Heiruna Doll|''Heiruna the Merciless'']]{{ref|name=ESOHD|[[Online:Heiruna Doll|Heiruna Doll]]'s dialogue in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}}}}

During Alduin's rule of Skyrim, a dragon named [[Lore:Kaalgrontiid|Kaalgrontiid]] split off from the bulk of his kin in order to sate his own pride and ambition.{{Ref|name=LADragons|{{Cite Book|Loremaster's Archive - Dragons in the Second Era}}}} He and his dragons came to rule of Elsweyr, where Kaalgrontiid planned to become the equal of [[Lore:Akatosh|Akatosh]] by consuming the power of the moon [[Lore:Jode|Jode]].{{ref|name=ESOKaalgrontiid|[[Online:Kaalgrontiid|Kaalgrontiid]]'s dialogue in [[Online:Online|ESO]]: ''[[Online:Elsweyr|Elsweyr]]''}} He and his army of dragons were ultimately trapped in the [[Lore:Halls of Colossus|Halls of Colossus]] by [[Lore:Khunzar-ri|Khunzar-ri]] and his four companions,{{Ref|name=KatD|{{Cite Book|Khunzar-ri and the Demons}}}} though he later emerged in {{Year|2E 582}}. Kaalgrontiid tasked his lieutenant [[Lore:Laatvulon|Laatvulon]] with restoring the worship of dragons. Laatvulon exploited the Khajiiti people's distraught over the chaos of the [[Lore:Planemeld|Planemeld]] and Interregnum, further made worse by the Dragons ravaging Elsweyr.{{Ref|name=NCAR|{{Cite Book|New Cult or Ancient Religion?}}}} Feeling that their gods have been absent, and seeing the god-like beings that dragons are, they looked to instead appease them for protection. As a result the [[Lore:Order of the New Moon|Order of the New Moon]], also called the New Moon Cult, was created, with the Khajiit Ra'khajin instated as Laatvulon's [[Lore:Dragon Priest|Dragon Priest]]. With the promise that the Order would ascend alongside their masters, and promising Ra'khajin the glory of becoming their spiritual leader as the [[Lore:Mane|Mane]], Kaalgrontiid gained a valuable asset in manpower for his operations.{{ref|name=ESOZayri|[[Online:Trial Master Zayri|Trial Master Zayri]]'s dialogue in [[Online:Online|ESO]]: ''[[Online:Elsweyr|Elsweyr]]''}} Kaalgrontiid eventually took command of the New Moon Cult and ordered them to do his bidding.{{ref|name=ESOAbnur|[[Online:Abnur Tharn|Abnur Tharn]]'s dialogue in [[Online:Online|ESO]]: ''[[Online:Dragonhold|Dragonhold]]''}} Through the combined efforts of [[Lore:Nahfahlaar|Nahfahlaar]], the reformed [[Lore:Dragonguard|Dragonguard]], and Queen [[Lore:Khamira|Khamira]]'s forces, Kaalgrontiid was slain and the Order of the New Moon were defeated.{{ref|name=ESONMR|Events of [[ON:New Moon Rising|New Moon Rising]] in [[Online:Online|ESO]]: ''[[Online:Dragonhold|Dragonhold]]''}}

During the time of the Interregnum, a group of aspirants took up residence in [[Lore:Dragonhome|Dragonhome]], an ancient Dragon Cult barrow in the northwestern corner of [[Lore:Karthald|Karthald Hold]] in Western Skyrim. The resurging cult, led by [[Lore:Aspirant Yolstagg|Aspirant Yolstagg]], believed they could summon a dragon and return the worship of the dragons to Skyrim.{{ref|name=ESOHD}}{{ref|name=ESOAY|[[ON:Aspirant Yolstagg|Aspirant Yolstagg]]'s dialogue in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} The Nord mage Heiruna the Merciless infiltrated Dragonhome intending to massacre the cultists,{{ref|name=HJ|{{Cite Book|Heiruna's Journal|ns_base=ESO}}}} but she was defeated and her soul was bound into that of a child's doll.{{ref|name=ESOHD}} The doll was later picked up by [[Lore:The Vestige|the Vestige]], who performed a ritual that let her reclaim her original human form. The two of them fought and were able to defeat the cultists.{{ref|name=SP|Events of [[ON:Spellbound|Spellbound]] in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}}{{ref|name=ESOHD}}{{ref|name=HTM|[[ON:Heiruna the Merciless|Heiruna the Merciless]]' dialogue in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} During this same time, a necromancer claiming to be a servant of the Dragon Cult resurrected the Dragon Priest [[Lore:Akiirdal|Akiirdal]] in [[Lore:The Howling Sepulchers|the Howling Sepulchers]] in [[Lore:Craglorn|Craglorn]].{{ref|{{Cite Book|Dragon Priest—Arise!|ns_base=ESO}}}}{{ref|[[ON:The Howling Sepulchers|The Howling Sepulchers]] location in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}}{{NewLeft}}

==Religion and Culture==
[[File:ON-texture-NordDungeonBannerDragon.png|thumb|right|200px|Dragon Cult Banner]]{{Quote Box|style=float:none; max-width:none|''There is no light without darkness. No fullness without hunger. No life without death. Such are the teachings of the great Dragons.''|[[Online:Dragonclash Face Marking|''Fire and Ash, by Fjot Tale-keeper'']]{{Ref|[[Online:Dragonclash Face Marking|Dragonclash Face Marking]] item description in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}}}}
Dragon Priests were usually called "Sonaak" by ordinary cultists,{{ref|name=ABP|{{Cite Book|A Brother's Plea}}}} which simply meant "Dragon Priest" in the language of the dragons.{{ref|[[SR:Dragon Aspect#Word Wall Translations|Dragon Aspect Word Wall translations]] in [[SK:Skyrim|''Skyrim'']]}} The highest ranking of these Priests were granted magical masks,{{ref|name=SKLoad|[[SR:Loading Screens|Skyrim loading screens]]}} usually made of differing metals and always possessing powerful enchantments.{{ref|[[Skyrim:Armor Artifacts#Dragon Priest Masks|Dragon Priest Masks]] in [[SR:Skyrim|''Skyrim'']]}} These Dragon Priests were powerful mages uniquely bonded in some magical, spiritual, or political way to a specific dragon,{{ref|name=THOZTS|{{Cite Book|The History of Zaan the Scalecaller}}}} who acted as intermediaries between men and their gods.{{ref|name=TDW}} Some Dragon Priest Masks seemingly refused to be removed from their wearers after death.{{ref|{{Cite Book|A Sister's Regret}}}}

Evidence suggests that the Dragon Priests crafted their armor out of the scales and bones of actual Dragons.{{ref|name=crafting|{{Cite Book|Crafting Motif 59: Scalecaller Style}}}} Lower-level worshippers also revered these remains, finding a powerful symbolic significance in the bones of their gods, and adorning themselves in the likeness of said bones.{{ref|[[Online:Dragonbone Chain Face Marking|Dragonbone Chain Face Marking]] item description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}}{{ref|[[Online:Dragonbone Chain Body Marking|Dragonbone Chain Body Marking]] item description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} Some Dragon Priests were also known to adorn their clothing with layered hide knots to protect themselves from both their enemies and the harsh weathers of Atmora and Skyrim.{{ref|[[Online:Frostbane Dragon Priest|Frostbane Dragon Priest]] costume description in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}} 

Clergy within the Dragon Cult below the level of Priest adorned themselves with the tall pointed diamond symbol known as the Sacred Rhombus, a design said to be symbolic of a Dragon's scales.{{ref|[[Online:Sacred Rhombus Circlet|Sacred Rhombus Circlet]] item description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} Some lower-level clergy of the Dragon Cult would wear metallic circlets stylized to look like a dragon with its mouth open, ''"ever-gaping in Eternal Hunger."''{{ref|[[Online:Eternal Hunger Coronal|Eternal Hunger Coronal]] item description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} Clergy members who wished to express their utter devotion and fealty to their masters were known as Dragon Vassals, and they would adorn their face and body with stylized markings to represent their devotion.{{ref|[[Online:Dragon Vassal Face Markings|Dragon Vassal Face Markings]] item description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}}{{ref|[[Online:Dragon Vassal Body Markings|Dragon Vassal Body Markings]] item description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}}{{ref|group=nb|While it is not explicitly stated that they are members of the clergy, since any word meaning "dragon" had its use restricted to Dragon Priests,{{ref|name=TDW}} it can be surmised that Dragon Vassals were part of the priesthood.}} 

Some faithful warriors within the Dragon Cult donned "war-masks" made of wood to wear into battle.{{ref|[[ON:Bleakrock Isle Antiquities#Cloven Ritual Mask|Cloven Ritual Mask]] antiquities codex entry in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}}

The Dragon Cult forbid any word for "dragon" be spoken aloud by anyone but the clergy themselves, themselves, though sources from the time often refer to dragons either as the ancient Nord "drah-gkon" or "dov-rha."{{ref|name=TDW}} Though these words were prohibited the proper names of specific Dragons were not; some cultists would carve small blocks with notches in the shape of the Dragon tongue to teach children the names of specific Dragons almost before they knew their parents' names. Dragons were certainly holy but they were also beloved, equally the subject of religious idols and childrens' toys to regular citizens living under Dragon Cult rule.{{ref|name=KTN|{{Cite Book|Kireth's Taarengrav Note}}}}

===Rituals===
[[File:ON-furnishing-Ruby Dragon Skull.jpg|left|thumb|150px|An effigy used during Dragon Cult sacrifices]]
Dragon Cultists were known to use dragon skulls as totems,{{ref|[[ON:Ancient Cultist Totem|Ancient Cultist Totem]] item description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} as well as create effigies of dragon skulls out of materials such as ruby, which scholars theorized may have been used as ritual focuses during sacrifices.{{ref|name=RDS}} Some cultists were known to sacrifice mortals to Alduin,{{ref|[[Online:Collectible Emotes#World-Eater's Fire Dance|World-Eater's Fire Dance]] item description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} and some scholars theorized that Alduin himself may have even administered rituals involving dragons themselves being sacrificed.{{ref|name=RDS|[[Online:Antiquity_Furnishings#Ruby Dragon Skull|Ruby Dragon Skull]] antiquities codex entry in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}} 

Dragon Cultists would annually gather together at the winter solstice to pierce their newest initiates with specially crafted stones claimed to be blessed by [[Lore:Alduin|Alduin]]'s own blood, turning them into rubies the shade of his eyes.{{ref|[[Online:Dragon Cult Piercings|Dragon Cult Eye Piercings]] item description in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}} Followers of Alduin were known to create [[Lore:Living Armor|Living Armor]], blessing it in the name of [[Lore:Alduin|Ald]], World-Eater, First of Dragons, so that his fire may empower and animate the armor to move on its own.{{ref|[[Online:Alduin Living Armor Senche|Alduin Living Armor Senche]] mount description in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}}{{NewLeft}}

===Temples and Tombs===
[[File:SR-place-Bleak Falls Barrow.jpg|right|thumb|Bleak Falls Barrow, a Dragon Cult tomb]]{{Quote Box|style=float:none; max-width:none|''They, who have given all to the Dragon Cult, who spend eternity in service to the Dragon priests. Their worship is endless. It continues even now, long after the fall of our ancestors. This should be heartening. It is a sign that though Skyrim may think of the Dragon Cult only as a distant memory, we will never truly be snuffed out.''|[[Lore:Draugr and the Dragon Cult|Dragon Cult Chronicler Skormvnir Kyrrund]]{{Ref|name=DADC|{{Cite Book|Draugr and the Dragon Cult}}}}}}
The dragons promised their priests eternal life,{{ref|name=DADC}} and after death they received it in the form of a unique type of [[Lore:Lich|lichdom]].{{ref|[[Skyrim:Valdar|Valdar]]'s dialogue in ''[[Skyrim:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} Unlike the lichdom of regular necromancers, Dragon Priests are sustained by the life forces of their dedicated cultists, who nightly rise as [[Lore:Draugr|Draugr]] who approach the priest's burial chambers and allow their life force to flow into the priest's body.{{ref|name=ATD|{{Cite Book|Amongst the Draugr}}}} Dragon Priests were notorious for hoarding treasure in the great tombs surrounding their burial chambers.{{ref|name=ND|[[ON:Narsis Dren|Narsis Dren]]'s dialogue in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} Entombed as they were, these Dragon Priests were said to be "dead but dreaming."{{ref|name=TESVSPOGG}}{{rp|471}}

Some Dragon Priests also had their own temples in life.{{ref|[[SR:Temple of Miraak|Temple of Miraak]] location in [[SK:Skyrim|''Skyrim]]}}{{ref|name=Frea}} In some cases, both a dragon priest and their bonded dragon lord would live in these temples alongside many living cultists.{{ref|name=ABP}}{{ref|name=THOZTS}} Both the tombs and the temples of the Dragon Cult were typically decorated with effigies to the ancient Atmoran and Nordic animal totems, not just those of the dragon.{{ref|name=EGTSkyrim|{{Cite Book|EGT|Skyrim}}}}{{ref|name=TODC|{{Cite Book|Temples of the Dragon Cult}}}} The inner sections of these structures were often sealed by elaborate vault doors, each one requiring its own key in the form of a special dragon claw sculpture.{{ref|[[Skyrim:The Golden Claw|The Golden Claw]] quest in ''[[Skyrim:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}

The Nords continued to venerate the temples{{ref|name=Jorunn}} and tombs{{ref|[[ON:Captain Rana|Captain Rana]]'s dialogue in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} of the Dragon Cult as dragonshrines well into the Second Era.

* [[Lore:Hasphat Antabolis|Hasphat Antabolis]] theorized that [[Lore:Ysgramor|Ysgramor]] may have been a Dragon Priest, while other more unorthodox scholars theorized he could even have been a dragon himself.{{ref|name=DWD|group=UOL|[[General:The Dragon War, documented|The Dragon War, documented]]}} This aligns with the fact Ysgramor is often described in Nordic legends as being able to Shout despite historically predating the Tongues taught by Paarthurnax, the "first mortal masters of the Voice."{{ref|name=PTHNX|[[Skyrim:Paarthurnax|Paarthurnax]]'s dialogue in [[SR:Skyrim|''Skyrim'']]}} Instances of Ysgramor Shouting in Nordic myth include him "Shouting Some Sense" into the Nords,{{Ref|name=FSKW|{{Cite Book|Five Songs of King Wulfharth}}}} him letting loose a "fearsome war cry that echoed across all the oceans" that was "said to be heard on the distant and chilling green shores of Atmora,"{{ref|{{Cite Book|Songs of the Return, Vol 7}}}} him having a "shout of thunder," and him fighting several [[Lore:Ice Wraith|Ice Wraiths]] with his voice.{{ref|group=UOL|name=Wulfric|[[General:Wulfric and the Snow Elf|Wulfric and the Snow Elf]]}} During Ysgramor's time, the only humans able to use the Voice would have been Dragon Priests,{{ref|name=TGATT|{{Cite Book|The Guardian and the Traitor}}}} granted that power directly by their Dragon Lords.{{ref|name=ZtS}} Also notably, Orthodox Dragon Priests were known to wear armor made from the remains of the Dragons themselves,{{ref|name=crafting}} with these pieces of armor sometimes being gifted to them by said Dragons as a sign of respect.{{ref|name=DRS}} Ysgramor was known to gift his most trusted [[Lore:Companions|companions]] armor made from dragon bones and scales.{{ref|[[Blades:Gauntlets of the Companions|Gauntlets of the Companions]] item description in ''[[Blades:Blades|Blades]]''}} One sacred chalice belonging to a member of the Ysgramor Dynasty prominently featured an image of Ysgramor himself surrounded by draconic silhouettes.{{ref|name=SCOY|[[ON:Eastmarch Antiquities#Sacred Chalice of Ysgramor|Sacred Chalice of Ysgramor]] antiquities codex description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}}
* The Dragon Priest [[Lore:Ahzidal|Ahzidal]] was born in [[Lore:Saarthal|Saarthal]] before the Night of Tears and helped Ysgramor's Companions during the Return,{{ref|name=Ahzidal}} and despite not immediately becoming a Dragon Priest continued to live until at least the beginning of Hakon One-Eye's quest to defeat Alduin.{{ref|name=miraak}} Though this seems to imply a very close timeline, Ahzidal was noted to be an immensely powerful and well-educated mage, and many mages were known to have become very long-lived via their use of magic.{{Ref|name=AT|[[ON:Abnur Tharn|Abnur Tharn]]'s dialogue in [[Online:Online|ESO]]}}{{ref|name=SA|[[Skyrim:Savos Aren|Savos Aren]]'s dialogue in ''[[Skyrim:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}{{ref|name=Alfe|[[MW:Alfe Fyr|Alfe Fyr]]'s dialogue in ''[[MW:Morrowind|Morrowind]]''}}
* Ancient Nord chronicles sometimes mention Dov-rha Warriors or Dov-rha Thanes, which scholars from the [[Lore:University of Gwylim|University of Gwylim]] connected to the later Dov-rha Axethanes of the [[Lore:First Empire of the Nords|First Empire of the Nords]].{{ref|name=DRS|[[ON:Dov-rha Sabatons|Dov-rha Sabatons]] antiquity codex entries in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} Since the use of the term "Dov-rha" was forbidden to all but Dragon Priests,{{ref|name=TDW}} it is probable that the original Dov-rha Warriors and Thanes were connected in some way to the Dragon Priesthood. Both Dragon Priests and [[Lore:Dov-rha Axethanes|Dov-rha Axethanes]] experienced special bonds with specific Dragons,{{ref|name=THOZTS}}{{ref|name=DRSI|[[ON:Dov-rha Sabatons|Dov-rha Sabatons]] item description in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} and both were known to wear armor made of dragon scales.{{ref|name=crafting}}{{ref|name=DRS}} Amalien of the University of Gwylim drew a connection between the (theorized) Dragon that gifted this armor to their bonded Axethane, and the Dragon [[Lore:Nahfahlaar|Nahfahlaar]] gifting his horn to the Khajiit hero Ja'darri,{{ref|name=DRS}} a mortal that Nahfahlaar shared an intimate bond with.{{ref|[[ON:Nahfahlaar|Nahfahlaar]]'s dialogue in [[ON:Online|ESO]]}} 
* Several [[Lore:Word Walls|Word Walls]] can be found around Skyrim, monuments written in the dragon language usually to commemorate a fallen hero. Many of these can be found in the temples and tombs of the Dragon Cult,{{ref|name=WW|[[SR:Word Wall|Word Walls]] in [[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]}} and many Dragon Priests were known to guard the secrets of these Word Walls.{{ref|name=CTA|Official description of ''[[Call to Arms:Releases#2021|Word Wall]]'' in ''[[Call to Arms:Call to Arms|Call to Arms]]''}}{{ref|name=WW}} Some of the inscriptions on certain walls date to well past the age of the Dragon Cult, with one wall raised in Skyrim dating to at least some time past the Yokudans' arrival in Hammerfell,{{ref|[[SR:Slow Time#Word Wall Translations|Slow Time Word Wall translation]] in [[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]}} though due to the fact the in-game locations of specific walls are determined by which words of the Wall's associated Shout the player has unlocked, it is unclear whether any specific Word Wall is or is not situated in a Dragon Cult temple or tomb.{{ref|name=WW}}
* The inhabitants of Skyrim during the Dragon War were variously labeled Nords{{ref|name=Paarthurnax|[[SR:Paarthurnax (dragon)|Paarthrunax]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} and Atmorans.{{ref|name=FSL2|{{Cite Book|First Servant's Letter, Page 2|ns_base=ESO}}}} The truth may lie somewhere in-between; while the two are considered distinct races,{{ref|name=TOoC|{{Cite Book|The Origin of Cyrus!|ns_base=Books}}}} the men living in the transition period between the terms' respective usage have been considered 'proto-Nords'.{{ref|name=FC|{{Cite Book|Frontier, Conquest}}}}{{ref|name=EGTSkyrim|{{Cite Book|EGT|Skyrim}}}}
