A [[Lore:Dragonborn|Dragonborn]] (or '''Dovahkiin''' in [[Lore:Dragon Language|Dragon Language]]) is a mortal blessed with the blood and soul of a [[Lore:Dragon|dragon]] by [[Lore:Akatosh|Akatosh]], the Father of Dragons and chief of the [[Lore:Nine Divines|Divines]].{{ref|name=TBotD|{{Cite Book|The Book of the Dragonborn}}}}{{ref|name=TRaFotB|{{Cite Book|The Rise and Fall of the Blades}}}} Some sources claim that Dragonborn are fragments of Akatosh's soul and share this status with [[Lore:Alduin|Alduin]] and possibly all of Dragonkind.{{ref|name=VonosJournal|{{Cite Book|Vonos' Journal}}}} Those with the blessing have an extraordinary aptitude in the use of the [[Lore:Thu'um|thu'um]]. They are able to absorb knowledge of shouts directly from the souls of slain dragons.{{ref|name=SRArngeir|[[Skyrim:Arngeir|Arngeir]]'s dialogue in ''[[Skyrim:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} This ability breeds fear in dragons,{{ref|name=SAT|[[General:The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim Announcement Trailer|''The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim Announcement Trailer'']]}} as the removal of their soul severs their immortality.{{ref|name=SRDelphine|[[SR:Delphine|Delphine]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}<noinclude>

The first known Dragonborn was [[Lore:Miraak|Miraak]], a [[Lore:Dragon Priest|dragon priest]] of the [[Lore:Merethic Era|Merethic Era]] who used the thu'um in an unsuccessful revolt against his masters.{{ref|name=TGATT|[[Lore:The Guardian and the Traitor|''The Guardian and the Traitor'']] — [[Lore:Books by Author#Lucius Gallus|Lucius Gallus]]}}{{ref|name=TharstanSR|[[SR:Tharstan|Tharstan]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]: [[Skyrim:Dragonborn|Dragonborn]]''}} The term is most heavily associated with the Dragonborn Emperors, a supposedly unbroken lineage of [[Lore:Cyrodiil|Cyrodilic]] [[Lore:Cyrodilic Emperors|monarchs]] beginning with [[Lore:Alessia|Saint Alessia]], a [[Lore:First Era|First Era]] [[Lore:Nede|Nede]] who entered into a divine Covenant with Akatosh.{{ref|name=TBotD}}{{ref|name=ToSA|{{Cite Book|Trials of St. Alessia}}}}{{ref|name=OtDF|{{Cite Book|Of the Dragonfires (Fragment)}}}}{{intnote|nb1|[nb 1]}}

==Characteristics==
[[File:SR-npc-Dragonborn.jpg|right|thumb|The Last Dragonborn absorbing a dragon's soul]]
Dragonborn are noted as "the ultimate dragonslayers" by the [[Lore:The Blades|Blades]], an [[Lore:Akaviri|Akaviri]] organization with expertise on the Dragonborn and dragonlore in general.{{ref|name=Esbern|[[SR:Esbern|Esbern]]'s dialogue in [[SR:Skyrim|''Skyrim'']]}}{{ref|name=TDE|{{Cite Book|Thalmor Dossier: Esbern}}}} They are said to be especially effective in combat against dragons, on a higher level than most mortals.{{ref|group=UOL|name=LttS|[[GEN:Look to the Skies – Hunting Dragons in The Elder Scrolls Online|''Look to the Skies – Hunting Dragons in The Elder Scrolls Online'']]}}{{ref|group=UOL|name=ToddGI|[[GEN:Todd Howard|Todd Howard]]'s comments in [https://archive.org/details/game-informer-issue-214-february-2011/page/56/mode/ ''Game Informer'', Issue 214]}}{{rp|57}} After slaying a dragon, Dragonborn may gain their knowledge, power, and life force by absorbing their soul, bypassing the traditional limitations and hindrances of learning the thu'um.{{ref|name=SRArngeir}}{{ref|name=SRUlfric|[[SR:Ulfric Stormcloak|Ulfric Stormcloak]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} Additionally, Dragonborn may gain knowledge of Shouts through [[Lore:Word Walls|Word Walls]], ancient [[Lore:Nord|Nordic]] monuments covered in [[Lore:Dragon Language|dragon-tongue]] runes which contain the power of the Voice.{{ref|name=CTA|Official description of ''[[Call to Arms:Releases#2021|Word Wall]]'' in ''[[Call to Arms:Call to Arms|Call to Arms]]''}} Dragonborn learn inscribed Words of Power as fast as the [[Lore:Greybeards|Greybeard]] masters.{{ref|name=SRArngeir}} Only a Dragonborn can unlock the true power of a Word Wall,{{ref|name=LfaF|{{Cite Book|Letter from a Friend|ns_base=Skyrim}}}} which calls out to them in an [[Lore:Aetherius|Aethereal]] song of dragon-tongue.{{ref|name=TSB|{{Cite Book|The Sublime Brazier}}}}{{ref|name=WordWalls|[[SR:Word Wall|Word Walls]] in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}{{ref|name=Prima|[[Books:The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim: Prima Official Game Guide#Legendary Edition|''The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim: Legendary Edition Prima Official Game Guide'']] — David Hodgson}}{{rp|241}}

Some Dragonborn are known to possess the gift of [[Lore:Sight|Sight]]. It was said that the dragon blood in the veins of members of the [[Lore:Septim Dynasty|Septim Dynasty]] allowed them to see more than lesser men.{{Ref|name=OBBaurus|[[OB:Baurus|Baurus]]' dialogue in ''[[Oblivion:Oblivion|Oblivion]]''}} Emperor [[Lore:Uriel Septim VII|Uriel Septim VII]] was troubled all his life by dreams that showed him visions of the future, including his assassination, the identity of the [[Lore:Hero of Kvatch|Hero of Kvatch]], and the start of the [[Lore:Oblivion Crisis|Oblivion Crisis]] that followed.{{Ref|name=OBUrielSeptim|[[OB:Emperor Uriel Septim|Emperor Uriel Septim]]'s dialogue in ''[[Oblivion:Oblivion|Oblivion]]''}} How he knew of the [[Lore:Nerevarine Prophecy|Nerevarine Prophecy]] and its significance was considered a central mystery of the time.{{ref|name=ALoUS7|{{Cite Book|A Life of Uriel Septim VII}}}} It was speculated that [[Lore:Martin Septim|Martin Septim]]'s recognition of his [[Lore:Fate|destiny]] and final plan to end the Oblivion Crisis could have been the product of a prophetic dream.{{Ref|name=OblivionPrima|[[Books:The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion Official Game Guide|''Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion Game of the Year: Prima Official Game Guide'']] — Peter Olafson}}{{rp|134}}

Famously, the Dragonborn are uniquely capable of performing a ritual to light the [[Lore:Dragonfires|Dragonfires]] of the [[Lore:Imperial City|Imperial City]]'s [[Lore:Temple of the One|Temple of the One]].{{ref|name=OBMartin|[[Oblivion:Brother Martin|Martin]]'s dialogue in ''[[OB:Oblivion|Oblivion]]''}}{{ref|name=ESORenald|[[ESO:Chevalier Renald|Chevalier Renald]]'s dialogue in [[ESO:ESO|ESO]]: ''[[ESO:Scalebreaker|Scalebreaker]]''}} The intricacies of the ritual are understood only by the Emperors of Cyrodiil,{{ref|name=OBJauffre|[[OB:Jauffre|Jauffre]]'s dialogue in ''[[OB:Oblivion|Oblivion]]''}} but it is known that the rekindling of the Dragonfires sustained the [[Lore:Liminal Barriers|Liminal Barrier]],{{ref|name=VonosJournal}} the dimensional protection between [[Lore:Mundus|Mundus]] and [[Lore:Oblivion|Oblivion]].{{ref|name=OBMartin}}{{ref|name=LB|{{Cite Book|Liminal Bridges}}}} The ritual required the [[Lore:Amulet of Kings|Amulet of Kings]], a divinely powerful [[Lore:Soul Gem|soul gem]] which could only be worn by those of the dragon blood.{{ref|name=ESOMannimarco|[[ESO:Mannimarco|Mannimarco]]'s dialogue in [[ESO:ESO|ESO]]}}{{ref|name=OBBaurus}} Due to their role in this process of maintaining the barrier between Mundus and Oblivion, the Dragonborn were said to "make the world whole"{{ref|group=UOL|name=ToddGI}}{{rp|57}} or "render [the] land alive".{{ref|name=Remanada|{{Cite Book|Remanada}}}}

Dragonborn are said to be destined for greatness.{{ref|name=ESOProphet|[[ESO:The Prophet|The Prophet]]'s dialogue in [[ESO:ESO|ESO]]}} The dragon blood gives them the innate urge to dominate and seek power, as well as an enhancement to their instincts.{{ref|name=PaarthurnaxSR|[[SR:Paarthurnax|Paarthurnax]]'s dialogue in ''[[Skyrim:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} Whether their abilities are a gift or a curse has been debated for centuries, with many having succumbed to the arrogance of their power.{{ref|name=SRArngeir}} Occasionally, Dragonborn may be transformed by their inner fire, becoming known as a Blaze of Akatosh and acquiring a power to be used as they see fit.{{ref|name=Blaze|[[ESO:Blaze of Akatosh|Blaze of Akatosh]] skin description in [[ESO:ESO|ESO]]}}

While dragon souls are generally believed to be beyond the means of mortal manipulation,{{ref|name=LADitSE|{{Cite Book|Loremaster's Archive - Dragons in the Second Era}}}} partial [[Lore:Soul Trap|soul traps]] have been known to work on Dragonborn.{{ref|name=Nelacar|[[SR:Nelacar|Nelacar]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}{{ref|name=Serana|[[SR:Serana|Serana]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]: [[Skyrim:Dawnguard|Dawnguard]]''}} While it has been asserted that a dragon cannot be resurrected by mortal means once their soul has departed,{{ref|name=LADitSE}} instances of divine resurrection have been recorded: Alduin used a Shout to bring his ancient horde back to life in the [[Lore:Fourth Era|Fourth Era]],{{ref|name=ABitD|[[SR:A Blade in the Dark|A Blade in the Dark]] quest journal entries in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}{{ref|name=Dragonstone|[[SR:Dragonstone|Dragonstone]] inscription in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} and, according to legend, [[Lore:Shor|Shor]] remade Wulfharth from his ashes in preparation for the [[Lore:Battle of Red Mountain|Battle of Red Mountain]].{{ref|name=FSoKW|{{Cite Book|Five Songs of King Wulfharth}}}} Furthermore, some Shouts are capable of summoning spiritual apparitions of Dragonborn, such as Call of Valor{{ref|name=JtSK|{{Cite Book|Jorunn the Skald-King (book)}}}} and Dragon Aspect.{{ref|name=Aspect|Appearance of [[SR:Ancient Dragonborn|Ancient Dragonborn]] in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]'': ''[[SR:Dragonborn|Dragonborn]]''}}

==Cultural Significance==
===Skyrim – The Dragonborn Heroes===
[[File:LG-back-Ebonheart Pact.png|thumb|left|The draconic insignia of the Ebonheart Pact]]
[[File:SR-concept-Dragonborn Miraak.jpg|right|thumb|Miraak, the first known Dragonborn]]
[[Lore:Skyrim|Skyrim]] is the origin of the known Dragonborn. The first known Dragonborn, Miraak, led the [[Lore:Dragon Cult|Dragon Cult]] in the Merethic Era.{{ref|name=Firstblade|[[ESO:Firstblade Style|Firstblade Style]] bundle description in [[ESO:ESO|ESO]]}} Though it may have been his destiny to defeat Alduin during the [[Lore:Dragon War|Dragon War]], he decided to pursue a different fate.{{ref|name=Prima}}{{rp|585}} He was sought out during the war for assistance, but declined,{{ref|name=MiraakSR|[[SR:Miraak|Miraak]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]: [[Skyrim:Dragonborn|Dragonborn]]''}} preferring to host his own rebellion out of his desire for power, betraying the dragons and devouring their souls.{{ref|name=CardLore|[[Legends:Card Lore#Miraak, Dragonborn|Card Lore - Miraak, Dragonborn]]}} After his defeat at the hands of Vahlok the Jailor and subsequent submission to [[Lore:Hermaeus Mora|Hermaeus Mora]], Miraak's name was erased from the histories, being remembered by the [[Lore:Skaal|Skaal]] as "the Traitor" in a cautionary tale.{{ref|name=TGATT}}{{ref|name=TharstanSR}}

The oldest Nordic tales tell of more Dragonborn rising to prominence during the age of dragons, becoming legendary figures and slaying several of the wyrms.{{ref|name=WhiterunGuard|[[SR:Whiterun Guard|Whiterun Guard]]'s dialogue during [[SR:Dragon Rising|Dragon Rising]] in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}{{ref|name=Costume|[[ESO:Dragon Warrior|Dragon Warrior]] costume description in [[ESO:ESO|ESO]]}}{{ref|name=Hero|[[SR:Hero of Sovngarde|Hero of Sovngarde]] dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} As dragons became scarcer, later tales maintained the importance of the Dragonborn, who used their Voices to defeat the enemies of Skyrim.{{ref|name=SRBalgruuf|[[Skyrim:Balgruuf the Greater|Jarl Balgruuf the Greater]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} Among these heroes was [[Lore:Wulfharth|Wulfharth]], a High King of Skyrim from the [[Lore:First Era|First Era]],{{ref|name=FSoKW|{{Cite Book|Five Songs of King Wulfharth}}}} believed by many to have been Dragonborn.{{ref|name=SRBalgruuf}}{{ref|name=Nels|[[OB:Nels the Naughty|Nels the Naughty]]'s dialogue in ''[[OB:Oblivion|Oblivion]]''}} His thu'um was notoriously powerful, preventing him from swearing into office.{{ref|name=FSoKW}} He fought many battles on Skyrim's behalf, such as the invasion of [[Lore:Morrowind|Resdayn]],{{ref|name=Sermon9|{{Cite Book|36 Lessons of Vivec, Sermon 9}}}} the expulsion of the [[Lore:Alessian Order|Alessian Order]], the war with the [[Lore:Orc|Orcs]] of the east, and the Battle of Red Mountain.{{ref|name=FSoKW}}

Later, in the long [[Lore:Interregnum|Interregnum]] of the [[Lore:Second Era|Second Era]], the Dragonborn warrior youth [[Lore:Tiber Septim|Talos]], allegedly known as Hjalti Early-Beard,{{ref|name=Loading|[[SR:Loading Screens|Loading screens]] in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} rose to prominence within Skyrim. It is said he took [[Lore:Old Hroldan|Old Hroldan]] from the [[Lore:Reachmen|Reachmen]] using his thu'um,{{ref|name=EydisSR|[[SR:Eydis|Eydis]]' dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}{{ref|name=GoOHSR|[[SR:Ghost of Old Hroldan|Ghost of Old Hroldan]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} and later studied under the Greybeards, gaining the title of Stormcrown.{{ref|name=SRArngeir}} Talos went on to become the first man to conquer all of [[Lore:Tamriel|Tamriel]], unifying it under the banner of the [[Lore:Third Empire|Third Empire]].{{ref|name=PGE1C|{{Cite Book|PGE|1|Cyrodiil}}}}{{ref|name=Brief1|{{Cite Book|Brief History of the Empire v 1}}}} It is said that, during [[Lore:Tiber War|his conquering]], when he met Nordic forces in battle at [[Lore:Sancre Tor|Sancre Tor]] who opposed a renewed Cyrodilic empire, they heard his thu'um and joined his cause, titling him "Skyrim's Son and the Heir to the Empires of Men".{{ref|name=PGE1C}} Upon his death, the Nords believe Talos was made a [[Lore:Talos|god]], the chief of their faith.{{ref|name=Heimskr|[[SR:Heimskr|Heimskr]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} Some obscure sources describe his role in the Nordic pantheon as a templeless Twilight God known as the Dragonborn God or the Dragonborn Conquering Son. They say the arrival of the Dragonborn God signals the coming end of the [[Lore:Kalpa|cycle]] and that he will be the only god to persist into the next.{{ref|group=UOL|name=TNTR|{{Cite Book|The Nords' Totemic Religion|ns_base=GEN}}}} The outlawing of his worship later caused the [[Lore:Stormcloak Rebellion|Stormcloak Rebellion]], a civil war in Skyrim.{{ref|name=SRUlfric}}

In {{Year|4E 201}}, the Greybeards summoned the first Dragonborn hero to High Hrothgar since Talos.{{ref|name=WhiterunGuard}} This hero became known as the Last Dragonborn. Their rise coincided with the [[Lore:Return of the Dragons|Return of the Dragons]], wherein several long-dead dragons came back to life to terrorize the province of Skyrim.{{ref|name=SRDelphine}} With the help of the Blades, they learned the secret to defeating Alduin on [[Lore:Alduin's Wall|Alduin's Wall]], a collection of ancient Akaviri dragonlore.{{ref|name=Wall|Events of [[SR:Alduin's Wall|Alduin's Wall]] in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}} Armed with the secret shout, Dragonrend, they entered the Nordic afterlife, known as [[Lore:Sovngarde|Sovngarde]], and defeated Alduin, saving Skyrim from his scourge.{{ref|name=Dragonslayer|Events of [[SR:Dragonslayer|Dragonslayer]] in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}

The Dragonborn became an important symbol in Skyrim, beyond any one individual's deeds. The [[Lore:Ebonheart Pact|Ebonheart Pact]] took a dragon as its insignia, honoring the Dragonborn of ages past as well as the broader Nordic struggle against the dragons.{{ref|name=Base|[[GEN:Elder Scrolls Online - Knowledge Base|''Elder Scrolls Online - Knowledge Base'']]}} They were also immortalized in song form; "The Dragonborn Comes" remained a popular bardic song across Skyrim into the Fourth Era.{{ref|name=Bards|[[SR:Bardic Performances|Bardic performances]] in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}

===Cyrodiil – The Dragonborn Emperors===
[[File:MER-art-The High Priestess (crop).jpg|left|thumb|Saint Alessia, the first Dragonborn Empress]]
[[File:MER-jewelry-Amulet of Kings.jpg|right|thumb|The Amulet of Kings]]
The saga of the Dragonborn Emperors began in {{Year|1E 242}}, when Slave Queen Alessia of the Cyrodilic Nedic tribes began [[Lore:Alessian Slave Rebellion|her rebellion]] against the tyrannical [[Lore:Ayleid Empire|Ayleid Empire]].{{ref|name=PGE3C|{{Cite Book|PGE|3|Cyrodiil}}}} The [[Lore:Daedraphiles|Daedraphile]] [[Lore:Ayleid|Ayleids]] called upon armies of [[Lore:Daedra|Daedra]] to assist in their defense, leading Alessia to seek help from the [[Lore:Gods|gods]]. According to legend, Akatosh (or by some accounts, [[Lore:Shezarr|Shezarr]]){{intnote|nb1|[nb 1]}} took pity on humanity and transformed a drop of his blood, a jewel known as Chim-El Adabal, into the Amulet of Kings,{{ref|name=TAoK|{{Cite Book|The Amulet of Kings}}}} an amulet and soul gem of immense divine power.{{ref|name=ESOMannimarco}} Akatosh blessed Alessia with the blood of dragons, transforming her into a Dragonborn, and together, they formed a Covenant: so long as Alessia's descendants wore the Amulet and rekindled great flames known as the Dragonfires, Mundus would never again be threatened by the Gates of Oblivion the Ayleids' armies emerged from.{{ref|name=TAoK}}{{ref|name=ToSA}}{{ref|name=TBotD}}{{intnote|nb2|[nb 2]}} Alessia won the war in {{Year|1E 243}},{{ref|name=TLKotA|{{Cite Book|The Last King of the Ayleids}}}} becoming the first Empress of Cyrodiil.{{ref|name=SatD|{{Cite Book|Shezarr and the Divines}}}} True to the Covenant made with Akatosh, Alessia's heirs wore the Amulet of Kings and ruled Cyrodiil for generations during the [[Lore:Alessian Empire|First Empire]].{{ref|name=TBotD}} Their rule came to an end in {{Year|1E 2331}}, when the [[Lore:War of Righteousness|War of Righteousness]] spelled the end of the Alessian regime.{{ref|name=OtWoR|{{Cite Book|On the War of Righteousness}}}}

Within the Amulet of Kings, the souls of the various Emperors who had worn it formed an [[Lore:Oversoul|Oversoul]], allowing the artifact to facilitate communication with the long-dead monarchs within.{{ref|name=OBProphet|[[OB:The Prophet|The Prophet]]'s dialogue in ''[[OB:Oblivion|Oblivion]]: [[OB:Knights of the Nine|Knights of the Nine]]''}} Several of the rulers within gave their testimonies of what occurred during the [[Lore:Middle Dawn|Middle Dawn]].{{ref|name=WWYWTDB|{{Cite Book|Where Were You ... Dragon Broke}}}} Despite the importance of the Amulet, the general populace was largely unaware of its power, giving its status as a sacred symbol of the Empire to the [[Lore:Red Dragon Crown|Red Dragon Crown]] instead, a mere piece of jewelry.{{ref|name=OBBaurus}}

In the years after the War of Righteousness, the Amulet of Kings was lost and the Dragonfires went dark. According to myth, their darkness was ended by King [[Lore:Hrol|Hrol]], who found and [[Lore:Sex|made love to]] the spirit of Alessia. The site at which they consummated became the mountain of Sancre Tor, where the infant [[Lore:Reman Cyrodiil|Reman Cyrodiil]] was found, wearing the Amulet of Kings, which burned with renewed fire.{{ref|name=Remanada}} However, this tale has been deemed dubious by some scholars of the Dragonborn, considering it to lack evidence, though Reman's dragon blood remained well established. Thus, Reman founded the [[Lore:Second Empire|Second Empire]], and his heirs perpetuated its rule for centuries.{{ref|name=TBotD}} Reman began a Cyrodilic tradition of hunting dragons for sport which continued through his heirs.{{ref|name=Ulfnor|[[ESO:Ulfnor|Ulfnor]]'s dialogue in [[ESO:ESO|ESO]]: ''[[ESO:Dragon Bones|Dragon Bones]]''}}{{ref|name=ESORenald}} Reman eventually came to be worshipped by [[Lore:Imperial|Imperials]] as the Worldly God.{{ref|name=VoF|{{Cite Book|Varieties of Faith...}}}} [[Lore:Reman Dynasty|His dynasty]] came to an end in {{Year|1E 2920}}, the last year of the First Era, when Emperor [[Lore:Reman III|Reman III]] and all his heirs were assassinated by the [[Lore:Morag Tong|Morag Tong]].{{ref|name=ESOAshur|[[ESO:Ashur|Ashur]]'s dialogue in [[ESO:ESO|ESO]]: [[ESO:Elsweyr|''Elsweyr'']]}}

The end of the Second Empire ushered in the Interregnum, a period of over four hundred years where no Dragonborn ruler emerged to light the Dragonfires. The Amulet of Kings went missing again amidst the wars of the petty-kings vying for power.{{ref|name=Remanada}} Eventually, [[Lore:Varen Aquilarios|Varen Aquilarios]], an Emperor in title who had captured the Imperial City, went on a quest in search of the Amulet, fearing he would never be accepted as a true Emperor without the dragon blood. His advisor, [[Lore:Mannimarco|Mannimarco]], told him of a ritual he could perform before the empty Dragonfires to rekindle them and convince Akatosh to make him Dragonborn. Forming a group called the [[Lore:Five Companions|Five Companions]], he successfully located and retrieved the Amulet, bringing it to the Dragonfire brazier to perform the ritual. However, Mannimarco had corrupted it, and instead of relighting the Dragonfires, Varen's incantation tore the veil between Mundus and Oblivion in an event known as the [[Lore:Soulburst|Soulburst]]. This enabled [[Lore:Molag Bal|Molag Bal]] to enact the [[Lore:Planemeld|Planemeld]], a Daedric invasion attempting to merge his domain of [[Lore:Coldharbour|Coldharbour]] with [[Lore:Nirn|Nirn]].{{ref|name=ESOProphet}} The calamity was eventually thwarted by [[Lore:The Vestige|the Vestige]],{{ref|name=ESOMeridia|[[ESO:Meridia|Meridia]]'s dialogue in [[ESO:ESO|ESO]]}} and the Soulburst subsequently became a cautionary tale that there were no shortcuts to power, particularly concerning Dragonborn.{{ref|name=ESORenald}}
[[File:LG-cardart-Avatar of Akatosh 02.jpg|left|thumb|The Avatar of Akatosh]]
The remainder of the Interregnum saw [[Lore:Battlefield Emperors|several petty-kings]] vie for the throne of Cyrodiil, frequently claiming forbearance from the Cyrodiil line to legitimize their claims.{{ref|name=TobiasRG|[[RG:Tobias|Tobias]]' dialogue in ''[[RG:Redguard|Redguard]]''}}{{ref|name=LtO|{{Cite Book|Letter to Orpheon}}}} Many awaited the arrival of a true Dragonborn Emperor with the divine right of rule to bring peace to the land.{{ref|name=ESORenald}}{{ref|name=DrakeESO|[[ESO:Drake of Blades|Drake of Blades]]' dialogue in [[ESO:ESO|ESO]]: [[ESO:Imperial City (DLC)|''Imperial City'']]}}{{ref|name=Grundwulf|[[ESO:Grundwulf|Grundwulf]]'s dialogue in [[ESO:ESO|ESO]]}} Eventually, a Dragonborn did appear: a young warrior named Talos, serving as a general under the [[Lore:Colovian Estates|Colovian]] warlord [[Lore:Cuhlecain|Cuhlecain]]. His thu'um proved useful to the warlord, who successfully captured Cyrodiil, alongside Talos recovering the Amulet of Kings from Sancre Tor.{{ref|name=TBoST|{{Cite Book|Battle of Sancre Tor}}}}{{ref|name=LoadingOB|[[OB:Loading Screens|Loading screens]] in ''[[OB:Oblivion|Oblivion]]''}} Before his coronation as Emperor, however, he was assassinated.{{ref|name=PGE1C}}{{ref|name=TAH|{{Cite Book|The Arcturian Heresy}}}} Talos, adopting the Imperial name Tiber Septim, took control of Cuhlecain's forces and claimed the throne of Cyrodiil for himself, ushering in the Third Empire and the [[Lore:Septim Dynasty|Septim Dynasty]] of Dragonborn Emperors.{{ref|name=PGE1C}}{{ref|name=TBotD}}

After the death of [[Lore:Pelagius I|Pelagius I]], the Imperial line moved from Tiber's heirs to those of his brother, [[Lore:Agnorith|Agnorith]].{{ref|name=Brief1}} For centuries, the Septim family maintained rule over Cyrodiil, though a few adopted or married members took the throne for intervals.{{ref|name=PGE3A|{{Cite Book|PGE|3|All the Eras of Man}}}} Regardless, every legitimate Emperor or Empress of the time was considered Dragonborn.{{ref|name=TBotD}} By the time of [[Lore:Uriel VII|Uriel VII]], a direct descendent of Tiber,{{ref|name=LoadingOB}} the Empire had entered a Golden Age not seen since his famed ancestor's rule.{{ref|name=Brief4|{{Cite Book|Brief History of the Empire v 4}}}} However, as was portended in his prophetic dreams, he and his sons were assassinated during {{Year|3E 433}}, when the [[Lore:Mythic Dawn|Mythic Dawn]] launched a coordinated attack on the family with the goal of darkening the Dragonfires.{{ref|name=OBUrielSeptim}}{{ref|name=OBJauffre}} Their success began the [[Lore:Oblivion Crisis|Oblivion Crisis]], when Gates to Oblivion opened up around the continent in an all-out invasion. Despite their planning, they did not account for [[Lore:Martin Septim|Martin Septim]], a bastard of the late Emperor he had secreted away years prior.{{ref|name=OBJauffre}} After gaining the Amulet of Kings, Martin raced to light the Dragonfires, but was too late to stop [[Lore:Mehrunes Dagon|Mehrunes Dagon]] from invading, destroying the veil between Oblivion and Mundus.{{ref|name=OBMartin}} In a final act of heroism, Martin shattered the Amulet of Kings, unleashing the power within and transforming him into a massive Avatar of Akatosh. The Avatar battled and banished Dagon from the world, and in its final act, permanently restored the Liminal Barrier.{{ref|name=OBMartin}}{{ref|name=TOC|{{Cite Book|The Oblivion Crisis}}}}

Though a few of the claimants to the Imperial throne who arose in the following [[Lore:Stormcrown Interregnum|Stormcrown Interregnum]] had legitimate claims, no one managed to establish rule for long. Unnatural storms plagued the Imperial City during the period, viewed by some as the punishment of the Divines. By the time [[Lore:Titus Mede I|Titus Mede I]] captured the throne, the rightfulness of his claim was considered unimportant compared to the good of escaping the mayhem of the interregnum.{{ref|name=RT4|{{Cite Book|Rising Threat, Vol. IV}}}} Despite this, Imperial law maintained that a living member of the Septim bloodline would have the right to become Emperor, seemingly overriding the [[Lore:Mede Dynasty|Mede Dynasty]], if they emerged in the Fourth Era.{{ref|name=StyrrSR|[[SR:Styrr|Styrr]]'s dialogue in ''[[SR:Skyrim|Skyrim]]''}}

===Others===
Second Era [[Lore:Altmer|Altmer]] prophecies spoke of the Dragonborn. Oracles from the [[Lore:Summerset Isles|Summerset Isles]] dismissed the notion that Tiber Septim was the mythical figure during his rise to power, but [[Lore:¥R|others]] on the [[Lore:Thalmor|Thalmor]] believed they were mistaken and urged the council to prepare for his coming conquest.{{ref|name=PGE1P|{{Cite Book|PGE|1|Preface}}}}

The [[Lore:Tsaesci (race)|Tsaesci]] of [[Lore:Akavir|Akavir]] invaded Tamriel in {{Year|1E 2703}}, searching for the Dragonborn of prophecy. They surrendered when they heard the thu'um of Reman Cyrodiil, telling him they had been looking for him and pledging him their service.{{ref|name=TRaFotB}}{{ref|name=TBotD}} They built [[Lore:Sky Haven Temple|Sky Haven Temple]] as a shrine to Reman, who they revered.{{ref|name=Esbern}}{{ref|name=AotD|{{Cite Book|Annals of the Dragonguard}}}} Within the temple, they constructed [[Lore:Alduin's Wall|Alduin's Wall]], a collection of their accumulated dragonlore and prophecies. In particular, the wall depicted the events laid out in the [[Lore:Prophecy of the Dragonborn|Prophecy of the Dragonborn]], which foretold the return of Alduin and the role the Last Dragonborn would play in his defeat.{{ref|name=Esbern}}
